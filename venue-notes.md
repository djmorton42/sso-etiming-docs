# Venue notes

Field notes for rinks where races are timed. Read the venue section before a meet when planning the camera, cables, desk, and network. After a meet, add anything the next crew will need: a working mount, a cable route, a network failure, or a change in the building.

Distances, lens choices, and placements below come from setup at that rink. A dash in the summary means that item has not been written down yet.

## Brampton

### Camera

There is no good place to put the camera. The obvious platform looks through the announcer booth glass, which blocks the shot.

## Cambridge

### Network

Venue Wi-Fi drops after about four hours and has to be reconnected. Apart from that dropout, it has been good enough for streaming.

### Cabling

Cables can be run around the back of the arena.

## Kingston

### Network

Venue Wi-Fi is poor.

### Timing desk

The timing desk sits on a raised porch.

### Camera

The camera is on the opposite side of the rink from the timing desk and uses a wide-angle lens.

### Cabling

The Ethernet run from the camera side to the desk is about 250 feet. It goes around the boards, through the netting, into a switch, and then up to the timing desk.

## Ottawa

### Timing desk

Wood benches.

### Transponder loop

The transponder wire did not read the full loop. Only the inner half responded. Interference is a possible cause; that has not been confirmed.

## Toronto

### Camera

The camera has to sit on the cross bar above the walkway. A mount for that bar still has to be built.

### Timing desk

The timing desk faces away from the rink. There is not enough room to set it facing the ice.

## Waterloo

### Network

Venue Wi-Fi is unusable. Use a phone hotspot for livestreaming and for any other internet access.

## Updating these notes

Edit the venue after a meet when the route, mount, desk, or network behaved differently than this page describes. Keep the note specific enough to set up from: which side of the rink, how the cable actually runs, and what failed.
